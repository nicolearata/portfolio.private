Case 01 · Influencer Marketing

Built an influencer engine on a shoestring.

A lot of brands overspend on single influencers, then lose faith. I believe in finding mass micro-influencers who are cost-effective and actually aligned with your brand and community — even when it takes hundreds of outreach messages, weekly shoot coordination, and heavy relationship management. It's about community and quality content, not virality.

Client
OLHSO by Shin Starr
Role
Strategy & Execution
Year
2024–2026
Budget
$6,670

The brief

OLHSO needed Bay Area visibility, and the standard playbook was clear: spend $5K on one mid-tier creator and hope for a hit. I've watched a lot of brands try that, get one underwhelming post, and walk away from influencer entirely. I think they're walking away from the wrong version of it.

The approach

The version of influencer that works is the slow version. Find creators whose communities actually overlap with yours. Treat them like long-term partners, not one-off transactions. Pay them fairly even when they're small. Coordinate around real product moments — not just "post a pretty picture."

That looks like hundreds of outreach messages to land 30 yeses, weekly shoot coordination across multiple creators at once, and the kind of relationship management that doesn't fit on a one-pager. Most of the work is invisible. The result is content that feels like word-of-mouth, not advertising.

  • Sourced and pitched well over 30 creators to land 30 confirmed bookings
  • Negotiated flat-fee deals averaging ~$220 per creator — well below mid-tier rate cards
  • Briefed each creator around timely product angles — giveaways, new menu items, and our collab with Bibigo
  • Layered the resulting content into our owned channels and review-request flow

The engine, year two

In 2026 I pointed the same playbook at OLHSO House's new lunch and to-go menu. I scheduled rolling influencer lunch visits tied to our review program, then hosted a curated five-creator experience with a companion guide I wrote so every creator knew the dishes, the story, and the details worth filming.

Beyond booking

I produce original creator content too.

Booking influencers is one thing. Directing custom content with named talent is another. I produced a "Mystery Items" video series with Chef Han — high-concept, on-brand, and built specifically to perform on social.

Case 02 · Strategy · Events · Partnerships

Reset the trucks' strategy to their best month ever.

OLHSO's robotic food trucks were built to cook meals en route to your door. It was a great story, but open cook-en-route service alone wasn't building a predictable business. I used my cost/profit model to make the case for a different one, then went out and booked it.

Client
OLHSO Trucks
Role
Strategy, Partnerships & Permitting
Year
2025–2026
Scope
2 trucks · SF + South Bay

The brief

By late 2025 I had a year of data from the trucks' first cost vs. profit model, and it said the same thing every month: roaming service was unpredictable, labor-heavy, and hard to plan prep around. The trucks needed fewer random days and more reliable ones.

The reset

In December I brought our COO and our Roaming Hunger partner a 2026 projection plan and a proposal: move the trucks off open roaming and onto four focused revenue modes.

  • Residencies — recurring, fixed locations with built-in foot traffic
  • Catering — booked, prepaid, and easy to prep for
  • Monthly "hero" events — festivals and large gatherings, chosen by the numbers
  • App pickup — keeping the tech story alive without driving the schedule

I also redesigned shifts across a two-truck model to split hours evenly and cut overtime, and built a monthly prep plan the kitchen could actually use.

Then I booked it

  • Secured Off the Grid residencies at SF Embarcadero and CSU East Bay, then expanded CSU with added weekly shifts
  • Wrote and submitted every permit for the new service areas: health permits, commissary verification, and inspections across SF and Alameda counties
  • Got the truck permitted into TechCrunch Disrupt 2025 in under two weeks, at an event that doesn't normally host food trucks, which led to coverage in TechCrunch and the AP
  • Launched the SF Embarcadero truck with a two-week multi-channel rollout, and worked the window myself in week one
  • Built an event pipeline with brewery pop-ups, college athletics, Cal Academy, and a year-round Event Application Tracker with timed deadlines
  • Handled the insurance side too: multi-carrier COIs for venues like the Presidio Trust, and automatic COI delivery to cut turnaround

Fewer random days, more reliable ones. The model told us where to go; my job was getting us booked there.

Case 03 · Product · Pricing · Sales

Launched three new revenue lines in one year.

Marketing a restaurant usually means filling the tables you already have. I spent 2026 building new ways for the brand to make money: weddings, private dining, and to-go. Each one needed a product, a price, and a sales process before it needed a campaign.

Client
OLHSO House + Trucks
Role
Product, Pricing & Sales
Year
2026
Lines
Weddings · Private Dining · To-Go

01 · Wedding catering

I built wedding packages from scratch, priced at about three times our standard per-serving price, and marketed them through Zola, bridal outreach, and a targeted Meta campaign. Then I ran our first wedding end to end: 75 guests at The Plant Connection SF. I handled the booking, invoicing, deposits, tastings, vendor coordination, and electric-chafer logistics, and served on-site the day of. It became our highest-margin event to date.

02 · Private dining patio

OLHSO House had a beautiful garden patio and no way to sell it. I wrote the pricing, booking terms, and cancellation policy, built the inquiry pipeline and SOPs, and designed the sales brochure and email outreach.

  • First bookings closed within weeks of launch, growing to 40-guest dinners and full buyouts
  • A Mother's Day prix fixe that opened the patio to smaller parties
  • A winterized, heated holiday patio with premium December pricing for peak season

03 · To-go, delivery & catering

I launched a bento box program across every channel: pickup, commission-free delivery through our own website (powered by DoorDash drivers), Cater2Me, and direct catering. I oversaw the menu, packaging, photography, and web pages, and integrated online orders with our POS so menus and prices match everywhere. Along the way we delivered our largest Cater2Me order ever: 250 bowls for Intuit.

Case 04 · Strategy · Ops · Infrastructure

Built the marketing function from zero.

When I joined OLHSO, there was no marketing infrastructure — no dashboards, no SOPs, no attribution, no calendar, no budget model. Two years later, there is. This is the case study about all the work that doesn't fit in a campaign recap.

Client
OLHSO by Shin Starr
Role
Solo Marketing Function
Years
2024 — 2026
Scope
House · Trucks · Micro-Restaurants

The brief

OLHSO is a multi-format Korean BBQ brand: a flagship restaurant, a fleet of robotic food trucks, and the first automated micro-restaurant KBBQ concept in the US. It needed marketing that could speak to all three formats at once — and someone willing to build the systems, not just run the campaigns.

The build

I treated year one as an infrastructure project disguised as a marketing role. Campaigns were the visible output; the real work was making it possible to run those campaigns repeatedly, profitably, and without me holding all the context in my head.

Infrastructure I shipped

  • Cross-channel attribution system — first-ever tracking tying Google, Yelp, influencer, and print spend back to app downloads, web traffic, and orders
  • Truck cost vs. profit model — the trucks' first P&L sheet, used to steer event selection toward profitable months
  • 2026 Marketing Budget — proposed and built from scratch to align spend with growth targets across House and Trucks
  • SOPs & SLAs — repeatable playbooks for catering intake, event quoting, post-event debriefs, review response, and kitchen comms
  • Annual truck event calendar — filled, with deadline pings and live-request alignment with kitchen prep
  • Monthly performance dashboards — recurring reports on ratings, CAC/ROAS, catering ROI, email/SMS revenue
  • Centralized marketing asset library — single source of truth for brand consistency across formats
  • Catering pipeline — intake → quote → contract workflow with pricing guardrails, plus DoorDash and Cater2Me integrations. Largest booking to date: our first wedding catering at $6K+
  • Review operations — audited 600+ reviews, standardized reservation language across every channel, and built a review-request SOP with a QR raffle and automated post-receipt emails
  • Event Application Tracker — a year-round calendar of events with timed application deadlines, so truck revenue is planned, not rushed

The campaigns get the press release. The systems get the next year of revenue.

Tradeshow Management

Three major activations. Each one under budget.

Tradeshows are where marketing meets logistics, sales, and crisis management — usually all at once. I project-managed NAMA Las Vegas (2025), AXC 2025, and AXC Nashville (2026) end-to-end: shipping, rigging, badge registration, utility orders, booth design, team scheduling, and on-site lead capture. AXC 2026 came in at $11,920 against a $13,515 budget, with a 1,400-word internal post-mortem feeding next year's planning.

Live Brand Activations

Producing the moments that drive the metrics.

Some marketing happens in feeds. Some has to happen in a room. I've produced in-person brand activations to drive product launches and category awareness — including a charcoal menu patio party at the House and a tasting event for the OLHSO truck. Each one combined vendor coordination, guest curation, on-site content capture, and real revenue at the door.

What I learned

The unglamorous work compounds. A budget spreadsheet built once and maintained quarterly is worth more than a campaign that goes viral and disappears. An SOP that runs every week without me is worth more than any one-off push. The job, eventually, is to build the engine and then make sure it runs without you in the room.

The kind of work I want to keep doing: bringing this builder's instinct to teams that need someone willing to write the docs, set the dashboards, and run the campaigns.

The brand

Stroke of the Wild started with a story I needed to tell. My maternal grandmother was displaced from Ramle, Palestine, in 1948 — and the brand grew out of a desire to honor that lineage through wildlife and "wild women" portraiture. The name itself comes from a comment I kept hearing from collectors: that I infuse movement in each line.

The brand voice is warm but unflinching. The visual world leans into texture, oil, gold leaf, and unfinished graphite. Every product page, every email, every social caption is written by me — and the consistency is the point.

The marketing

I built and ran the entire growth function as a side project — meaning every dollar of marketing had to pay for itself. No team, no budget cushion, just the work.

  • Grew an audience to 2,500+ across Instagram and TikTok in 1.5 years
  • Partnered with 24 influencers, reaching 40K Instagram followers and 757K TikTok viewers in one year
  • Conducted 48 customer interviews to build a real audience profile — drove 10% content engagement rate
  • A/B tested email subject lines and grew open rates 30% in three months
  • Built the storefront, the visual system, the photography, and the copy myself
